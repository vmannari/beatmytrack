# Music craft guide

Use this when diagnosing or designing music. It is a compact reasoning aid, not a formula.

## 1. Identity before variation
A track needs something the listener can recognize: a motif, contour, harmonic gesture, groove, texture, or instrumental signature. It does not need to be a pop hook.

Ask:
- What returns?
- What changes?
- What is the listener expecting after the first phrase?
- What later version makes the earlier material feel newly meaningful?

## 2. Phrase logic
Prefer intentional phrase relationships over stochastic note selection.

Useful structures include:
- statement -> answer -> departure -> return
- tension -> delay -> resolution
- stable phrase -> altered ending
- motif -> fragmentation -> reconstruction

Silence/rests are useful when they create direction. Random gaps are not automatically subtle.

## 3. Expectation and surprise
Musical pleasure often depends on balancing prediction and deviation. Use repetition to establish expectations; vary timing, harmony, voicing, contour, orchestration or resolution selectively.

Avoid both extremes:
- everything changes, so nothing becomes meaningful;
- nothing changes, so attention turns into fatigue.

## 4. Rhythm and driving
A clear pulse is not the enemy. Mechanical insistence is.

Moderate syncopation and human timing can create motion without an intrusive grid. The racing line, braking point and grip limit must remain more important than the music's accents.

## 5. Development
Intensity can change through:
- register
- harmonic openness/tension
- rhythmic activity
- articulation
- counterpoint
- instrumentation
- width
- phrase length
- resolution strength

Do not use loudness and instrument count as the only progression controls.

## 6. Background function
A good game cue may be less impressive in isolation than in context. Protect the frequency/time space used by engine, tyres and impacts. Repeated play matters more than first-listen spectacle.

## 7. Diagnostic vocabulary
When feedback says “bad,” identify the dimension:
- **composition:** weak motif, poor harmony, no direction
- **performance/timbre:** fake attack, grating tone, over-distortion
- **groove:** stiff, busy, dragging, repetitive
- **arrangement:** too empty, too dense, role conflict
- **mix:** masking, harshness, level imbalance
- **adaptive:** transitions feel arbitrary, punishment-like, or discontinuous
- **fatigue:** acceptable once but irritating across repeated runs

Do not solve one category by randomly modifying another.
