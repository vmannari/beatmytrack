# Launch, validation and live-service plan

## Positioning hypothesis
“Build a circuit. Develop your car. Beat your friends.” Demonstrate actual active driving and one clear time challenge. Do not sell a generic promise of hundreds of cars, a supposedly competition-free market, or screenshots of features not implemented.

## Zero-paid-acquisition sequence
P0: recruit ten varied phone players beyond the developer. Observe steering, first completion and voluntary retries; do not lead them toward praise. P3: observe ten first-time creators; measure time to a valid shared track and recipient completion. P4: recruit 30–50 private online players to exercise uploads, sparse boards, identity recovery and reports. P7: aim for 300 real first-time external players with a complete seven-day observation window before commercial conclusions; expand toward 1,000 only after stability and retention gates.

Recruit through personal invitations, appropriate indie/racing communities where promotion is allowed, voluntary small-creator collaborations and shareable challenge clips. These require labour even when no cash is spent. Track the source and avoid treating friends or incentivized testers as representative organic players. No assumed influencer coverage or guaranteed viral multiplier belongs in the financial forecast.

## Store and sharing assets
Prepare one short unedited driving clip, six honest device screenshots, a gameplay-first trailer, clear free-progression wording and a support page. Lead with driving, then creation and competition. Explain offline versus online features. Track received challenge -> preview -> install/open -> first completion -> returned challenge. Offer a readable challenge code as a fallback; do not assume install-deferred deep links work without implementation/testing. QR codes point to a hosted challenge reference, not a promise that an entire replay always fits in a QR.

## Community seeding without fake popularity
Ship a quality official track pool. Invite a small creator cohort and credit accepted circuits. League selection uses geometry/abuse validation, test drives, difficulty and class suitability, not merely likes. Never fabricate racers, times, star ratings or histogram populations. Do not reward creators financially or with unlimited currency per click; that invites manipulation.

## Weekly owner routine
Review crash/ANR and sync/verification backlog daily during early release. Review moderation queue at a published service cadence; public UGC must not open without a responsible operator. Once a week: freeze/test the next three events, review the previous settlement, restore a sample backup, check cloud spend and replay CPU/traffic, review fairness/economy outliers, then publish a short change note. Do not introduce new physics mid-season without a documented compatibility/correction plan.

## Monthly product review
Compare cohorts, not just cumulative installs: first-race completion, D1/D7/D30, voluntary races, legitimate play hours, time-to-upgrade, creator activation, successful shares, percentage buying capacity, refund rate, MAU service cost, moderation minutes and owner hours. Separate acquisition channels and app versions. Small samples mean wide uncertainty; predeclare an experiment and review window. Do not count delayed purchases as monthly recurring subscriptions.

## Funding and marketing decision
Paid acquisition remains zero in the baseline. Use measured cohort net receipts minus attributable service/refund/support cost to estimate acquisition headroom. The workbook's initial purchase yield per install is NOT full measured LTV and is not permission to buy ads at that price. Require mature cohorts, a conservative margin and payback timing before a capped acquisition experiment. Current model has no guaranteed revenue from advertisements.

## Shutdown / maintenance mode
If revenue fails to cover the approved cash/time budget, stop new spending first, retain offline gameplay, communicate online changes, allow draft/track export, settle open competitions and honour or resolve purchase commitments appropriately. A one-time hosted-capacity purchase must not imply unlimited resources forever. Define migration/retention and consumer-facing terms before sales, with qualified review. No lifetime hosting promise is approved here.
