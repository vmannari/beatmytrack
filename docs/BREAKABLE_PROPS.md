# Hay bales and breakable wooden crates

Owner request, 2026-10-12. BP01: hay bales lose shape and shed harmless hay. BP02: their remaining core becomes smaller and lighter. BP03: wooden crates move under light contact and accumulate damage under heavier crashes. BP04: broken crates leave movable wood pieces with small driving effects. BP05: exact replay/rewind, per-attempt reset, five ordinary laps, local storage and pinned contracts. BP06: original shared models/icons, actual captures and bounded cost. Preserve G01/R01/T01-T04/V04-V06/Q01.

## Behaviour

Choose **Objects → Props → Hay bale** or **Wooden crate**. The existing `crate` identifier is retained, so saved crates receive the new behaviour. Both support the established place/move/rotate/size/duplicate/delete/undo/save flow and are available in all five biomes. Neither is baked into an impassable native wall. No transient destruction state is saved into a track document.

A hay bale starts as a tied 2 × 1.2 m bundle. Contacts at at least 1 m/s shed material and expose progressively frayed, uneven shapes. The remaining fraction controls actual mass (85 kg at standard size, proportional to remaining volume) and the core's linear scale/radius (cube root of the remaining fraction). Object size scales mass by volume. A worn bale is easier to push and causes less car speed loss. After the final small remainder disperses, the core has no collision. Loose hay has **zero mass and zero car, collision, grip or tyre-contamination effect**; its short outward movement and eventual ground scatter are presentation carried in the deterministic state.

A standard wooden crate has a 22 kg effective mass. Gentle contacts move/turn it without reducing integrity. Relative impacts above 4 m/s accumulate damage; repeated heavier crashes open/split boards, then remove the crate core and release six planks. Distinct impacts are latched until separation: sustained contact or following an already-moving crate at the same speed does not count as a new crash every tick. Reverse contacts push in the actual direction of travel. Wood pieces slide, rotate and settle, remain across laps, and can be pushed again. Their oriented planar contact produces a small momentum-based speed loss (at most 0.035 m/s per piece/contact tick) and slight off-centre yaw response. Wood does not become an oil-like grip patch or an impassable wall. Ground objects do not contact an airborne or elevated car.

Damage and scatter persist across laps. New attempts reset them completely, including immediate intact visual restoration during the countdown before the first driving tick. Rewind restores integrity, impact latches, core positions/velocities, individual fragments and their motion; resumed physics reproduces the exact later branch. Existing collision observer output supplies crash feedback without introducing an external audio dependency. Mass/damage thresholds and the small wood response remain owner-review balance values.

## Implementation and contracts

`BreakableProps` extends the existing canonical light-prop path using only ordered numeric arrays and the fixed 120 Hz step. Other light props retain their exact previous path. There is no 3D rigid-body solver, runtime random generator, wall clock, network or file operation in the simulation. Up to 96 authored crates can produce 576 physical planks. Cosmetic loose hay is capped at 128 clumps; replacing old hay never discards physical wood. The conservative combined stress bound is 704 fragments. Moving pieces check native solid scenery; settled pieces avoid world queries. Fragment-to-fragment and crate-to-crate rigid-body collisions, punctures and structural vehicle damage are outside this slice.

Three original prepared damage meshes per kind are shared across instances, with one shared matte sRGB material. Loose hay and wood use two MultiMesh batches with original fibre/plank geometry. The existing one-shot palette renderer uses the same intact models. Rendering changes visibility, scale, transforms and buffers only; it does not mutate canonical state. Prepared scenery clones preserve object bindings and isolate mutable MultiMesh buffers.

Physics was advanced from `arcade-planar-0.54` to the breakable slice's `0.55`; the shared tree subsequently advanced to `0.56`, which is preserved. Compiler is `creator-compiler-26`.
