The left workshop now has a fabricated steel bench with foot pads, bolted joints, lower drawer storage, grained timber worktop, framed perforated board and shaped tools. Two spanners sit alongside a ratchet, pliers, claw hammer, measuring square, two screwdrivers and a varied socket rail. The mounted vice has a swivel base, machined/serrated jaws, spindle and sliding handle. The tyre rack has cross braces, flanged shelves, feet and bolts; complete spare wheels now use shaped alloy spokes, drilled brake details and moulded sidewall rings. This changes only garage spare wheels, not the vehicle models or their canonical geometry.

The compressor is a smoothly shaded domed tank on a wheeled frame, with cooling fins, motor casing, regulator, actual pressure dial/ticks/needle, hose coil and identification plate. Curved surfaces use indexed lathed geometry with per-vertex normals, avoiding the first faceted-tank appearance. The right drawer cabinet has inset fronts, rounded recessed pulls, fasteners, side vents, kick plates, worktop/back lip and castors. Storage cases have reinforcing ribs, protective corners, lid seams, latches, hinges and open carry handles; the counter case retains its previous shelf-clearance envelope. Shelf supports now have fixing rails, slots, bolts and diagonal brackets. The original poster receives layered frame profiles and corner hardware.

Shelf contents are purposefully different. The bottom shelf carries different-sized oil bottles, a handled coolant can and service tins; the middle shelf has a storage case, an open parts bin with small hardware and an open brake-parts package; the top has a woven tool bag, taped carton and differently sized tins. Positions and heights vary by collection. Repeated drawers, rack hardware and sockets remain functional sets, while the previous duplicated four-item shelf rows and repeated wrench grid are removed. There are no new inventory, repair, tuning or reward mechanics.

Original cached shader finishes provide brushed alloy, powder-coated steel, cast metal, moulded plastic, rubber and woven fabric, plus a separate timber-grain shader. Painted walls, shutter panels and trophy-display backing/storage receive matching finish detail. Colour pigments use source-colour uniforms; detail attenuates with projected size and stays restrained. Roughness and surface-normal response distinguish the materials. Concrete is now mostly dielectric rather than metallic, with subtle micro-normal and roughness variation; the existing limited analytic roof-light reflection approximation remains. These are procedural opaque materials, not imported bitmap/normal-map assets or reflected room/car geometry. No external media/provider/dependency or runtime AI is added.

Geometry remains prepared into the existing full-room cache/material batches; primitive and lathe meshes are shared where their shape matches. The final inspected room has **59 mesh instances, 193,038 triangles and 204,199 vertices**, all finite. This includes the architecture/trophy/roof furnishings, not just the newly marked props. Carousel environments retain their lighter prepared geometry and frozen actual car renders. Camera/zoom, roof cutaway, identical turntables, car light sweeps, local paint/dirt and the trophy-camera flow remain.
