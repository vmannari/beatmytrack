# Modular car assets — 31 October 2026

The car constructor can restore an offline-baked stock assembly and attach individual fitted-kit scenes. The six selectable vehicles have 20 stock assemblies covering full/simple detail and supported editable/fixed modes; four cars have 242 non-stock kit modules across both detail levels. Meshes are shared, while paint and other mutable material roles are rebound per car. The existing procedural constructor remains the fallback for missing, incompatible or stale assets and for initially fitted simple cars, whose stock shadow proxies do not represent fitted geometry.

The existing live kit-replacement path also uses compatible individual modules. Wheel and steering pivots remain separate; Hovercraft fan rotors are replaced with their associated housings. Personal ghosts can reconstruct a detached rest pose from modular assets when no in-memory rest template exists.

The build identity includes a conservative per-car model-source revision, excluding the generated assemblies and modules themselves. A source checkout also checks model and shader hashes. An engine, configuration, or source mismatch causes a procedural fallback. Asset generation must be repeated after relevant model or rendering changes; build identity generation alone deliberately does not certify stale generated assets.

## Performance and limits

One alternating Windows headless Compact test with four fitted parts recorded procedural construction at 711.555, 174.174 and 174.453 ms and modular construction at 143.908, 30.339 and 29.461 ms. The first pair includes additional first-use work; the later pairs are warm, and the host was shared. These are component wall times, not frame-rate, phone or cold-driver measurements. The modular path uses no cooperative construction yields: its measured construction time is synchronous, and source hashing is present in this checkout and omitted in packaged builds. **The synchronous attachment can still block a loading frame**, and the existing maximum cooperative slice counter does not measure it. No Android performance, thermal, memory-peak or gameplay FPS result is claimed.

The generated scenes occupy approximately 32 MiB on disk before package compression. A simple, initially fitted car uses the existing constructor to preserve its correct shadow geometry; live changes on an editable simple car can use the prepared modules. The rendering comparison and CPU geometry tests exercise desktop/headless cases and do not replace owner or physical-device acceptance.

## Validation

The final focused matrix passes 1,033 checks, including selected combinations, mutable-material isolation, wheel and fan articulation, shadow casters, ghosts after the car moves, and a process-local invalid fourth module that forces a complete procedural fallback after three successful module attachments. The existing Kart kit suite passes 994 checks; the Compact incremental-kit suite passes 346; driving passes 72; personal ghosts pass 38; and the procedural cooperative-construction fixture passes 18. All runs used the crash-dialog wrapper and isolated offline profiles. They printed a Windows root-certificate-store diagnostic.

Separate exhaustive runs cover each non-stock module at both quality levels, comparing the changed assemblies against procedural controls and asserting actual module hits: Compact 1,265/0, Kart 1,010/0, Forklift 538/0 and Hovercraft 1,286/0. The final Android Debug export-pack audit found all 262 scenes with byte-identical SHA-256 hashes and included the compiled loader, ghost and identity scripts. This verifies package contents, not execution on an Android device.

The retained [decorated Compatibility comparison](evidence/modular-car-assets-2026-10-31/decorated-comparison.json) compares Compact, Kart and Hovercraft with fitted parts, pattern, sticker and weathering. The optimized and procedural 640×360 views differ in 2–16 pixels; procedural repeat controls themselves differ in 0–15 pixels. Some isolated channel deltas are large, so the captures are not byte-identical. Geometry, UVs and vertex colors matched in the measured fitted triangle comparisons; maximum corresponding-normal difference was approximately 0.015°. The focused test permits up to 0.02° while checking geometry, UVs, colors, materials and shadow geometry.

At the time of this modular-asset pass, `test_racing_car_models.gd` reported 89 checks and two Forklift failures involving shadow casters and wheel frames. A [control copy](evidence/modular-car-assets-2026-10-31/racing-models-procedural-control.gd.txt) with both modular paths disabled reproduced the same failures and measurement rows. The subsequent [Forklift shadow and steering-test fix](FORKLIFT_SHADOW_PROXIES_2026_10_31.md) addresses both findings.

## Rebuilding

Generate the client build identity with `scripts/build_identity.ps1`, bake `res://tests/bake_modular_car_bases.gd`, and bake `res://tests/bake_modular_car_kits.gd`, using the pinned engine and `scripts/run_no_crash_dialog.py` for each noninteractive Godot launch. Regenerate the identity afterward and verify that the modular revisions did not change; generated asset files are excluded from those revisions. Use an isolated `APPDATA` and empty `BMT_API_URL` when running these offline utilities. The individual bakers support vehicle filters, and the kit baker also supports quality and category filters.

The focused regression is `res://tests/test_modular_car_assets.gd`; its `--profile` mode reports construction timing and actual base/module hits. `res://tests/capture_modular_car_ab.gd` provides fixed-camera visual comparisons. Logs, captures, initial failures and stage probes from this pass are under `build/modular-car-tests/`, `build/modular-car-visual/`, `build/modular-car-bake/` and `build/modular-car-base-work/`.
