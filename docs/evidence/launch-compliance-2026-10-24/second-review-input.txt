The revised terms are materially better. Most of the issues from my first review have been addressed, particularly the purchase descriptions, player-created content, shutdown rights and liability clauses.

However, I found two significant new concerns and several remaining launch requirements.

2. Issues I would still change
Critical
 Account deletion and purchased entitlements — §12
This clause concerns me:
Deletion permanently closes the account and erases its entitlement records, including unused allocations and monthly/permanent ad-free access.

The situation is particularly problematic with Google Play.

Permanent ad removal is normally implemented as a non-consumable purchase. Google describes these purchases as permanently associated with the purchasing Google account and unavailable for repurchase. 


developer.android.com



Consequently, a player could:
1. Purchase permanent ad removal.
2. Delete the Beat My Track account.
3. Create a new game account using the same Google account.
4. Find that their paid benefit is gone, but Google Play still considers the product purchased.
My recommendation: Support verified restoration of non-consumable entitlements to a new game account, with safeguards against duplicate activation.

This need not restore deleted tracks or spent publishing slots.

I would also avoid promising to erase every transaction record. Some records may need lawful retention for accounting, fraud prevention or outstanding consumer claims.
Important
 Publishing slots — §8
There is a significant change from the model we previously discussed.
Earlier agreement	Revised terms
One free publishing slot each month	One initial free publishing slot
Unused monthly slots do not accumulate	No monthly allocation

Was removing the monthly free slot intentional?

The revised terms also state that every new published revision consumes another slot.

I would reconsider this. Charging another slot to correct a small mistake in a published track could be commercially unpopular and potentially surprising.

My preference would be:
- First publication consumes a slot.
- Editing the existing published track does not consume another slot.
- Publishing a separate new track consumes another slot.
If your intention is to charge for every revised publication, that must be unmistakably disclosed at the point where the player confirms the revision.
Important
 Age and international markets — §3
The 13+ policy is a reasonable starting point, but it does not independently establish compliance throughout the EU, US and Canada.

For the US, the FTC distinguishes general-audience services from child-directed services and gives specific guidance on neutral age screening and situations where the operator learns that a user is under 13. Merely stating an age limit in the terms is insufficient if the actual service falls within COPPA requirements. 


Federal Trade Commission



For Canada, the Privacy Commissioner issued new children's privacy guidance on 20 October 2026. It emphasises meaningful consent and appropriate protections for teenagers, particularly around social interaction and data sharing. 


priv.gc.ca



Quebec also requires special attention. Its standard-form contract rules generally require a French version to be supplied before the consumer expressly chooses another language. Your revised text recognises this, but an English-only launch does not resolve it. 


oqlf.gouv.qc.ca



I would require a country-sensitive age, consent and advertising design before release.
Important
 Ad-free benefits and competition — §8
The revised ad-free benefit is attractive:
- Automatic interruption ads disappear.
- Players receive the ordinary race resumes without viewing rewarded ads.
- The limit remains three resumes per race.
That is much clearer.

Two matters remain:
1. If banner or other advertisements exist, clarify whether they are also removed. Otherwise, "ad-free" may be misleading.
2. Confirm whether races using resumes remain eligible for official records and Weekly Rivalry rankings. If resumes affect competitive results, the game rules should explain this equally to paying and non-paying players.
Important
 Moderation procedures — §11
The wording is acceptable as a foundation, but the Digital Services Act may require more specific operational arrangements.

For hosting services within its scope, Traficom identifies requirements covering receipt of illegal-content notices, decisions, reasons for restrictions and disclosure of automated decision-making. Some additional platform obligations have exemptions for micro and small enterprises. 


Traficom



If you use automated filtering of names or tracks, disclose its relevant role and available human review appropriately.

The terms should accurately describe the moderation mechanisms you actually implement.
3. Remaining publication blockers
These are mostly unfinished details rather than structural problems:
- §1: Publisher identity, contact information and legal-page URLs.
- §9: Classification of purchases, withdrawal process and functional refund arrangements. Finland's electronic withdrawal-function requirement has applied since 19 June 2026 to relevant online contracts. 
  
  
  KKV
- §11: Final reporting and appeal contact.
- §14: Planned shutdown notice period and communication channels.
- §17: Final support languages and legally appropriate regional documents.
These should be completed before the respective features or markets go live.
4. What I would retain
The improvements to §§4, 7, 13–16 and 19 are good.

In particular:
- Allowing monetised gameplay videos is sensible for a game relying on community growth.
- Separating player-created layouts from supplied game assets protects your intellectual property.
- Excluding unrelated marketing and AI training from the ordinary content licence is fair.
- The revised shutdown clause is substantially more defensible.
- Removing the "as is" disclaimer improves clarity.
- The successor-operator provisions should help if you eventually transfer the game to an Oy or sell the business.
Overall verdict
Legal drafting quality
Strong

The remaining work is primarily around commercial decisions and compliance implementation.

Publication readiness
Not yet

Account-deletion consequences, withdrawal processes, age-related compliance and remaining placeholders need resolution.

My strongest recommendation is to fix the account deletion/purchase recovery interaction before building further commerce features. That is the most consequential new issue in this revision.

I would also confirm the change from a monthly free publishing slot to a single initial allocation before finalising §8.

Once those product decisions are settled and the launch processes are implemented, the terms should be close to a version suitable for final jurisdiction-specific legal approval.
