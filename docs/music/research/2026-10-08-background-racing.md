# Background and racing-score research - 2026-10-08

MUS34/MUS20-MUS33/Q01. Owner: "This is like someone learning to play piano, so it
is again a no. You need to look into music theory, inspect working patterns for
game soundtracks especially what works on the background and in racing games."

The clear-theme study is rejected. This report is research and a revised
composition brief, not another audio replacement. Sources below are first-person
composer accounts, published theory and original research. They document useful
practices; they do not prove a universal recipe or our own musical quality.
Reference recordings were not copied, downloaded or used as conditioning. No
acoustic listening to reference tracks is claimed in this session.

## What the working examples establish

**Trackmania: develop the racing feel first.** Elliot Callighan describes starting
with racing material and carrying its character into the title music. This is a
particularly relevant case because of the game's racing/creator context.
[Composer's account, part1](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/writing-music-for-the-world-of-trackmania-1-of-3-).
For Beat My Track, establish the groove/ensemble character before treating a
piano tune as the foundation of the whole soundtrack.

**Trackmania: background roles need restraint.** Its editor music was revised
after Nadeo found the original too melodic and expansive; the final arrangement
reduced melodic, harmonic and swelling activity.
[Composer's account, part2](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/writing-music-for-the-world-of-trackmania-2-of-3-).
Our menus and creator sessions need musical continuity with modest foreground
events and enough internal detail to reward listening.

**Trackmania: variation is authored inside the groove.** Callighan discusses
refining individual parts/transitions and building another cue around a bass
figure with purposeful gaps.
[Composer's account, part3](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/writing-music-for-the-world-of-trackmania-3-of-3-).
Variation should change an ending, response, articulation or accompaniment;
unrelated notes and arbitrary tiny timing offsets do not supply musical direction.

**Ori: pulse and atmosphere can coexist.** Gareth Coker describes continuous
gameplay pulses with softened attacks, evolving string textures and space for
sound effects. He also describes extensive testing in the game.
[Coker's first-person interview](https://composer.spitfireaudio.com/en/articles/gareth-coker-the-music-of-ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps).
This account covers Will of the Wisps and continuity with Blind Forest. Transfer
those principles into a racing ensemble; it does not authorize copying Ori's
melodies or imply that its quieter title-screen mood fits our races.

**Forza: intensity can use fewer competing events.** Cohen and Nielsen describe
the2023 score's more minimal intense sections and the emotional role of moving
players into and out of races.
[First-person composer Q&A](https://www.sknr.net/2023/11/27/we-talk-forza-motorsport-with-composers-kaveh-cohen-and-michael-nielsen/).
Our high state should have a clear musical focus, rather than every instrument
continually making a new gesture.

**Forza7: the band sound is part of the composition.** In the label's2017 release,
the composers describe a vintage-rock ensemble concept and emphasize players,
instruments, rooms and recorded tone.
[Original press release reproduced by its publicity account](https://www.maintitles.net/forum/discussion/4225/four-forza-motorsport-soundtrack-release/).
This is primary production testimony, not independent evidence of popularity.
The useful lesson is to treat phrasing, guitar articulation, kit character and
ensemble sound as essential work, rather than assuming correct pitches render a
convincing rock performance.

## The theory that matters for this brief

| Concept | Meaning | Beat My Track application |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse and syncopation | A regular beat can support accents between beats. | Stable kit; bass/guitar anticipate or answer selected accents. A few intentional gaps keep motion elastic. |
| Motive | A short recurring gesture with recognizable rhythm/contour. | Restore a small identity inside the groove. It need not be a foreground piano line through every bar. |
| Phrase direction | Establish an idea, repeat/answer it, develop it and return or conclude. | Two-bar gestures in four/eight-bar phrases, with recognizable altered endings. |
| Harmonic rhythm | The rate at which chords change. | Let harmony provide stable terrain; increase movement at a deliberate lift rather than changing for its own sake. |
| Voice leading | How each chord voice moves into the next. | Hold shared tones and move selected voices smoothly for atmospheric continuity. |
| Register and texture | Where parts sit, and how many gestures compete. | Bass/kit/guitar supply motion; quieter sustained colour supplies depth; one focus takes the foreground at a time. |

The sentence model develops a recurring idea through continuation; pop/rock
theory also describes statement/response/departure/conclusion modules.
[Sentence](https://openmusictheory.github.io/sentence.html),
[pop/rock form](https://openmusictheory.github.io/popRockForm-containers.html).
These explain relationships between ideas. Merely labelling eight bars as a
sentence does not establish that their rhythm, contour or performance is effective.

Pop/rock frequently combines straight and syncopated parts, and its harmonic
practice differs from classical syntax.
[Syncopation](https://openmusictheory.github.io/syncopation.html),
[rock harmony](https://openmusictheory.github.io/popRockHarmony.html).
Classical part-writing offers a useful common-tone continuity technique, but its
strict parallel-motion rules are not a required style for rock guitar voicings.
[Voice-leading teaching text](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/VoiceLeadingFourPartsRootPosition.html).

Two studies provide limited supporting evidence. Witek et al.'s66-participant
drum-break survey found strongest movement/pleasure ratings at intermediate
syncopation; it was not a racing-performance test.
[Original2014 study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0094446).
Cassidy/MacDonald report125 driving-game participants and poorer results for
experimenter-selected high-arousal music than self-selected music. Only the
abstract was available here; this does not isolate an optimal tempo or prescribe
a genre.
[Original2009 abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/102986490901300207).
160 BPM remains a working target from the owner's direction, not a scientifically
proven optimum. Faster tempo, density and aggression are separate decisions.

## Why the previous implementations missed the brief

These are code/score findings and diagnostic interpretations, not new ear judgments.

1. The spectral race opening removed much of the intended melodic register. A
   player received bass fragments rather than an intentionally written foundation.
2. The generated performance never established that it followed its MIDI score.
   Retiming an estimated pulse did not solve theme or phrase fidelity.
3. The direct sketch overcorrected toward a continuous foreground piano tune.
   Its planned role RMS targets place piano11.285dB above drums and9.702dB above
   lower guitar doubling. The full-cue melody is only1.94dB below the complete
   mix. That supports the owner's description of a piano-led result.
4. Its melody events use one fixed nominal piano gain; lower guitar doubles each
   event. The arrangement lacks purposeful alternation of foreground roles and
   phrase-shaped expression. Recorded samples and small timing variations alone
   do not establish believable ensemble performance.
5. Major-key notes, a faster clock, named sections and additional layers were
   treated as solutions without establishing a compelling groove and sound.
6. Loop/headroom/source-hash tests were allowed to dominate delivery evidence.
   They are necessary technical checks and cannot decide atmosphere, sporting
   character, annoyance, musical identity or repeated-play comfort.

## Revised composition brief

The next piece should be a rhythmic rock underscore with atmospheric depth. This
is our proposed application of the research, not a claim that a new cue exists.

- Establish a confident, playable bass/kit/guitar groove immediately at a
  nominal160 BPM/4-4. Use an interlocking two-bar figure with intentional gaps,
  restrained transients and variation over four/eight-bar phrases.
- Embed a three-to-five-note signature gesture in bass/guitar responses. Leave space between
  complete statements; use rhythm, altered endings and later register changes
  to develop it. Piano is occasional colour or a response.
- Give warm electric guitars appropriate picked/muted/ringing articulations.
  Bass and kick share important anchors; snare/hat/ghost notes have a consistent
  pocket. Phrase-shaped dynamics, connected releases and real tone matter.
- Use held/common-tone support, suspended/additional-note colours and small inner
  voice movements for depth. Soft string/analog texture should widen the space
  without turning the cue into a lyrical piano performance.
- Reserve a broader sustained melodic focus for exceptional driving. Underlying
  groove and identity remain usable at every state. Avoid establishing mood by
  deleting the musical idea from the low state.
- Derive menus from the successful racing identity with fewer foreground events,
  gentler percussion and reduced harmonic movement. Creator sessions require
  particular attention to repetition fatigue.

| Qualifying run | Intended musical role |
|---|---|
| 0-6s | Groove and brief identity establish confidence and anticipation. |
| 6-16s | Bass/guitar responses complete the rhythmic conversation. |
| 16-30s | Firmer kit and phrase variations add drive. |
| 30-44s | Theme becomes more evident through sustained/answering material. |
| 44-56s | Guitar width and quiet counterpoint broaden the known material. |
| 56-60s | Earned lift introduces the reserved melodic focus. |
| 60-90s | Sustain flow through small internal changes and the existing PB breath. |

At160 BPM one bar is1.5s; two bars3s; eight bars12s;40 bars60s. Existing confidence
thresholds and next-bar/two-bar fades can reach the exceptional peak around60s.
This is a timing calculation, not an instruction to give the payoff for elapsed
time alone. Keep real sector/PB/rival signals and current setback/rewind rules.

Author actual musical-role parts against the same chord/phrase timeline. Each
playable state must be intentional. Spectral filters of a full mix do not replace
orchestration. Write end/begin harmonic motion and releases for repeated play;
use the validated waveform guard where needed. A finish sting may resolve more
strongly than a continuous gameplay loop.

## Production and evaluation order

First establish the groove, tone and instrumental identity as a complete racing
cue. Then assess phrase development and atmospheric depth, both alone and with
real car audio. Build adaptive parts and menu/biome arrangements from that cue.
An exposed piano transcription can check notes but is not the production target.

Acceptance needs listening in actual imperfect/clean/PB driving and repeated
restarts: attention remains on racing, car feedback is intelligible, the groove
feels coordinated, changes feel intentional and high-state payoff belongs to the
same piece. Coker's implementation account documents gameplay video with musical
change annotations as a useful handoff method.
[Implementation Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/behind-the-music-crafting-the-score-for-ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-part-2).

No new generation, audio installation, game UI/physics/backend changes, device
check/test or deployment occurred in this research pass. Acoustic reference
analysis, a satisfactory composition and owner/device acceptance remain open.
